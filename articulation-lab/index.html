Natural: dragging the tongue also follows a smooth pedagogical jaw/lip/root coordination field built from the vowel targets.

Current synth acoustics respond directly to tongue body position, jaw opening and lip rounding. Lip spread and tongue-root retraction are visual/model-state axes in this alpha; stronger acoustic coupling is deferred until a more principled tract model is used.

Approx. IPA is the nearest vowel anchor in this synth’s own modeled F1/F2/F3 space. It is a teaching hint, not automatic phonetic transcription of a human voice.

Try /æ/ vs /ɑ/ — then /i/ vs /u/
The v0.5 teaching targets give /æ/ stronger lateral lip spread and a front-low tongue, while /ɑ/ uses a more posterior tongue/root configuration. In alpha.2, strong rounding also visibly narrows the mouth opening, especially near /u/.
Mouth Synth = continuous synthesis
In Natural, dragging the tongue also moves jaw/lips/root using a smooth interpolation of the vowel teaching targets. In Independent, every control stays separate so you can explore unusual combinations deliberately.
Evidence boundary
These normalized postures are pedagogical. Human anatomy varies, multiple articulations may lead to similar acoustics, and the cavity shading is not a measured area function.
