Japanese buttons use one Japanese speaker’s public-domain recordings. The source contains very short repeated kana tokens, so v0.4.3 detects an actual voiced token, keeps a small onset/offset margin, then applies pitch-preserving time-stretch. The resulting button vowels are about 0.39–0.41 s of audible material rather than a longer file dominated by silence.

English buttons are human recordings from redistribution-safe sources. Most still use the CC0 IPA reference set, but KIT /ɪ/ now comes from the vowel nucleus of a real US-English kid recording. The /k/ aspiration and /d/ closure are cut away; the ~0.11 s vowel is intentionally not time-stretched, so its natural short-vowel character remains.

Processed button levels are active-RMS matched for easier comparison while respecting peak headroom. Male / female / child language-matched sets are added only when recording rights and speaker metadata are explicit.
